"about four": above 4.0 and at most 4.5 small requests. The test does not fail on it, but it
is not a clean pass. The 0.5 margin is the test's reading of "about"; the spec gives no margin.

Measured with the goal, as spec rev 29 words them (no goal changed)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
- The size-limit pause. A commit that arrives while the maintenance checkpoint at the WAL size
  limit (64 MiB) switches the generation waits for that switch. Measured: 339 ms [330 ms] at
  50 ms added, that is 5.5 [5.3] small requests. This is the longest wait of all small
  commits. No reader was open; with readers that stay open the pause can be longer. Task 18
  puts the measured value in the README; the README does not state it yet.
- The 10% change commit waits for the upload of its own log record, about 102 MiB: 2.63 s
  [3.09 s]. The spec (rev 29) words this as about 2.7 s. That is one large request.
- In phase 1 every generation switch came in a maintenance run: each 10% change (about
  102 MiB of WAL) passes the 64 MiB limit. No small commit of phase 1 did a switch. Phase 1b
  measures the switch inside an app commit on its own.
- Phase 1b: 4.25 to 4.39 small requests over 6 commits in 2 runs. The four requests are the
  seal-start record, the head move, the WAL header record and the commit's own log record;
  the rest is the local fsync and the engine work. The longest generation switch extra wait
  in phase 1 was 126 ms [131 ms].
- Phase 1c: each try filled the queue to 1000 commits behind (1000 small commits in about
  60 ms, as each returns after the local fsync), then a PASSIVE checkpoint, then one commit.
  That commit waited for 16 records of about 62 commits (the spec counts about 57 commits per
  record and about 18 uploads), then the three requests. The 6 tries in 2 runs waited 1033 to
  1264 ms. The worst is within 3% of the bound of about 1.3 s.

Restore detail (goal 2), run 1
------------------------------
- buckwal::restore returned at 300 ms: 7 GETs, 3 log objects, a 1 MiB block fetched.
- The writer open of the restored directory returned at 640 ms, with blocks not yet local
  (Choice C). The background fetch made the file fully local (app.db-blocks removed) at
  3.4 s from the restore start. All 262,144 rows were there.

Pack run detail (goal 3), step times of run 1
---------------------------------------------
- 3a, kept copy: read 634 ms, apply 1,431 ms, upload 9,145 ms, verify 56 ms, publish 83 ms;
  210 MB down, 1,076 MB up.
- 3b, new pack directory: read 110 ms, apply 4,206 ms, upload 9,256 ms, verify 58 ms,
  publish 80 ms; 1,286 MB down, 1,076 MB up.
- The upload of the 1 GB pack takes about 9 s at this store's speed (about 118 MB/s).

Two packers (goal 4)
--------------------
- Both runs started together from two new pack directories. One run moved the pointer (22.7
  to 24.3 s); the other ended as superseded and deleted its own pack and codes.
- GC with a 5 s trim grace and pack keep time: 2 passes in 15 s. After it, pack/ held only the
  newest pack and its codes; the bucket held 7 objects: head, pack-latest, the pack, its codes
  and 3 log objects of the head's generation. No pages/ or manifest/ object, no orphan pack,
  no open multipart upload aborted.

Limits of this measurement
--------------------------
- Local RustFS, not a cloud store: the delay is per request, the bandwidth is local (about
  118 MB/s up). On AWS S3 from this Mac the upload speed was 12.4 MB/s (docs/packer-recon.md),
  so a 1 GB pack upload there takes about 80 s, and goal 3 on such a link misses 30 s. Real
  AWS runs belong to the coordinator and were not made.
- The size-limit pause was measured with no reader open.
