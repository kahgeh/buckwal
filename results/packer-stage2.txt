The restart wait (goals 1b and 1c)
----------------------------------
- The rule (spec rev 33): the goal for a commit that restarts the WAL is its request count
  plus at most 1.5 small requests. The unit is the median small PUT of the same run.
- 1b. The six commits that restarted the WAL with batching Off waited 237 to 278 ms: 4.08 to
  4.45 small requests in run 1 and 4.25 to 4.51 in run 2. The worst is 0.99 small requests
  under the goal of 5.5. The time above four small requests is not measured: the test does
  not break the commit into its parts.
- 1c. The six counted tries waited 1232, 1094 and 1157 ms [1347, 1249 and 1269 ms]. The worst
  try of run 1 (21.21 small requests) is 74 ms under its goal of 1306 ms; the worst of run 2
  (21.81) is 42 ms under its goal of 1389 ms. The queue just before the commit was 1000, 936
  and 1000 [1000, 1000 and 1000] commits behind, and 15 to 17 records of about 59 to 63
  commits landed before the switch. No try had fewer than 900 commits behind, so every try
  counted.

Measured with the goals (no goal value for these)
-------------------------------------------------
- The size-limit pause. A commit that arrives while the maintenance checkpoint at the WAL size
  limit (64 MiB) switches the generation waits for that switch. Measured: 319 ms [338 ms] at
  50 ms added, that is 5.5 [5.5] small requests. This is the longest wait of all small
  commits in phase 1. No reader was open; with readers that stay open the pause can be
  longer.
- The 10% change commit waits for the upload of its own log record, about 102 MiB: 3.00 s
  [2.88 s]. The spec words this as about 2.7 s. That is one large request.
- In phase 1 every generation switch came in a maintenance run: each 10% change (about
  102 MiB of WAL) passes the 64 MiB limit. No small commit of phase 1 did a switch. Phase 1b
  measures the switch inside an app commit on its own. The longest generation switch extra
  wait in phase 1 was 127 ms [133 ms].

Two packers (goal 4)
--------------------
- Both runs started together from two new pack directories. One run moved the pointer (25.1 s
  [23.6 s]); the other ended as superseded (25.9 s [25.3 s]) and deleted its own pack and
  codes.
- GC with a 5 s trim grace and pack keep time: 2 passes in 15 s [15 s]. After it, pack/ held
  only the newest pack and its codes; the bucket held 7 objects: head, pack-latest, the pack,
  its codes and 3 log objects of the head's generation. No pages/ or manifest/ object, no
  orphan pack, no open multipart upload aborted.

Limits of this measurement
--------------------------
- Local RustFS, not a cloud store: the delay is per request, the bandwidth is local (about
  120 MB/s up). On AWS S3 from this Mac the upload speed was 12.4 MB/s (docs/packer-recon.md),
  so a 1 GB pack upload there takes about 80 s, and goal 3 on such a link misses 30 s. Real
  AWS runs belong to the coordinator and were not made.
- The size-limit pause was measured with no reader open.
