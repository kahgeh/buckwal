"about four": above 4.0 and at most 4.5 small requests. The test does not fail on it, but it
is not a clean pass. The 0.5 margin is the test's reading of "about"; the spec gives no margin.

The two misses
--------------
- 1b. All six commits that restarted the WAL waited 251 to 282 ms. Measured against one small
  request, that is 4.60 to 4.71 [4.04 to 4.55] small requests. The round 1 runs (7da944c)
  measured 263 to 273 ms, 4.25 to 4.39 small requests ("about four"), with one small request
  at 62 ms. The waits in ms are nearly the same in all runs; the unit (58 to 62 ms at the
  start of phase 1, 64 to 65 ms when phase 1b starts) moves the verdict. The time above
  four small requests is not measured: the test does not break the commit into its parts.
- 1c. The six counted tries waited 1222, 1228 and 1273 ms [1325, 1268 and 1161 ms]. The
  worst try of run 1 is 4.6% above its bound; the worst of run 2 is 1.6% above its bound.
  The queue just before the commit was 1000, 1000 and 978 [1000, 944 and 936] commits behind:
  1000 in three of six tries, 936 to 978 in the other three. 15 to 17 records of about 59 to
  63 commits landed before the switch (the spec counts about 57 commits per record and about
  18 uploads). No try had fewer than 900 commits behind, so every try counted. The round 1
  runs measured 1033 to 1264 ms, all within that bound.

Measured with the goal, as spec rev 29 words them (no goal changed)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
- The size-limit pause. A commit that arrives while the maintenance checkpoint at the WAL size
  limit (64 MiB) switches the generation waits for that switch. Measured: 336 ms [343 ms] at
  50 ms added, that is 5.8 [5.5] small requests. This is the longest wait of all small
  commits in phase 1. No reader was open; with readers that stay open the pause can be
  longer. Task 18 puts the measured value in the README; the README does not state it yet.
- The 10% change commit waits for the upload of its own log record, about 102 MiB: 2.67 s
  [3.03 s]. The spec (rev 29) words this as about 2.7 s. That is one large request.
- In phase 1 every generation switch came in a maintenance run: each 10% change (about
  102 MiB of WAL) passes the 64 MiB limit. No small commit of phase 1 did a switch. Phase 1b
  measures the switch inside an app commit on its own. The longest generation switch extra
  wait in phase 1 was 131 ms [139 ms].

Two packers (goal 4)
--------------------
- Both runs started together from two new pack directories. One run moved the pointer (24.3
  to 25.0 s); the other ended as superseded and deleted its own pack and codes.
- GC with a 5 s trim grace and pack keep time: 2 passes in 15 s [17 s]. After it, pack/ held
  only the newest pack and its codes; the bucket held 7 objects: head, pack-latest, the pack,
  its codes and 3 log objects of the head's generation. No pages/ or manifest/ object, no
  orphan pack, no open multipart upload aborted.

Limits of this measurement
--------------------------
- Local RustFS, not a cloud store: the delay is per request, the bandwidth is local (about
  121 MB/s up). On AWS S3 from this Mac the upload speed was 12.4 MB/s (docs/packer-recon.md),
  so a 1 GB pack upload there takes about 80 s, and goal 3 on such a link misses 30 s. Real
  AWS runs belong to the coordinator and were not made.
- The size-limit pause was measured with no reader open.
